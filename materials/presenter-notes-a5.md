# Presenter notes - Leader session 5: The weekly business review

**Length:** 45 min · **Audience:** CxO, VPs, founders · **Mode:** thinking, no code

## Run of show
- **0-3 · Welcome.** Frame: "a tree on a wall is a diagram; a tree walked every week is a management system." Cadence beats heroics.
- **3-22 · Part 1, what a WBR is.** Walk the three-box flow SVG: dashboard to owner-narrates to logged decision. Hammer the two non-negotiables: trailing-12-weeks + YoY, and owners narrate their own branch. Give the Amazon origin as evidence, not decoration.
- **22-42 · Part 2, running it well.** Lead with the tree not the slide. Drill the three-part question - which driver, which segment, versus what baseline - and have the room say it back. Name the four anti-patterns out loud. Tell the churn-caught-six-weeks-early story as the payoff.
- **42-45 · Part 3 + Q&A.** Weekly (operational, driver-level) vs monthly (strategic, roll-up). Tee up a6.

## Preflight
- Have a real weekly dashboard screenshot ready - ideally with a trailing + YoY view.
- Pre-pick one anti-pattern the room is guilty of; naming it live lands harder than the abstract list.
- Confirm both SVGs zoom cleanly on the projector.

## Never cut
- Trailing-12-weeks + YoY - without baselines there is no signal-vs-noise, and the review collapses.
- Owners narrate their own branch - the ownership rule is the cultural core.
- The three-part question - it is the entire method spoken aloud.

## Cut if long
- The Amazon origin self-study card (mention in one sentence).
- The "match cadence to metric speed" self-study aside.
- Trim the four anti-patterns to the two the room actually suffers.

## Likely exec questions
- **"Weekly feels like too much overhead."** The overhead is one hour that prevents quarter-end scrambles. The cadence is the product; fifty small corrections beat one big one.
- **"What if nothing moved this week?"** Then it is a five-minute meeting. The value is being present when something does move - you cannot predict which week.
- **"Who should own a branch nobody wants?"** Assign it anyway; an unowned branch is why problems hide. Ownership can be provisional and revisited.
- **"How do we stop it becoming status theatre?"** Lead with the tree, demand an action with owner + date on every deviation, and ban 'we should look into that.'
- **"Isn't this just a KPI meeting?"** No - a KPI meeting reports numbers; a WBR walks the tree, routes each move to an owner, and ends in decisions.
