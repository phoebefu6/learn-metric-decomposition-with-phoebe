# Presenter notes - Leader session 6: The monthly leadership summit

**Length:** 45 min · **Audience:** CxO, VPs, founders · **Mode:** thinking, no code

## Run of show
- **0-3 · Welcome.** Frame the finale: "the weekly keeps operators sharp; the monthly is where metrics meet strategy." Promise governance + Goodhart + where-next.
- **3-22 · Part 1, the monthly roll-up.** Walk the roll-up SVG: four weekly trees into one narrative into logged decisions. Drill the four questions - on plan, what moved and why, what are we doing, what do we need from you. Sell narrative over dashboards, decisions over updates.
- **22-42 · Part 2, governance + Goodhart.** One definition per metric. Guardrails by design - walk the signups-WITHOUT-activation SVG. Deliver Goodhart's Law in one sentence and give gaming vs vanity examples. Tie to OKRs as where Goodhart bites hardest.
- **42-45 · Part 3 + close.** Pre-read, exceptions-only, decisions logged. Point to the three sibling courses. Give the honest-scope note. Close the leader track.

## Preflight
- Have one real gamed-metric story from the room's own world ready (a green OKR that hurt the business).
- Line up the three sibling course links so the handoff is concrete, not vague.
- Confirm both SVGs zoom; the guardrail one is the emotional beat, make it land.

## Never cut
- Goodhart's Law in one sentence - the single most durable idea in the course.
- The guardrail pairing (target + a number it must not harm) - the practical defence.
- The honest-scope note - reading metrics is not building the platform; credibility depends on saying so.

## Cut if long
- The "whole leader track in one line" recap card.
- The pre-read mechanics detail (state it in one sentence).
- Trim the sibling-course descriptions to titles only.

## Likely exec questions
- **"Isn't every KPI a target? Are all metrics doomed?"** No - the fix is guardrails and rotating what you target. A measure only rots when it is the sole thing rewarded.
- **"Monthly and weekly feel redundant."** Different altitudes: weekly corrects drivers fast, monthly decides strategy. The weekly is the evidence the monthly argues from.
- **"How many guardrails per target?"** Usually one or two - the outcomes most at risk from pushing that lever to an extreme.
- **"Where do OKRs actually fit?"** The tree and the OKR are the same shape; key results ARE targeted metrics, so pair them with guardrails from the start.
- **"You said you don't teach building the platform - so where do I learn that?"** learn-data-warehouse for sources, learn-business-intelligence for dashboards, learn-strategic-thinking for the strategy layer.
