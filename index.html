SEC-02 WHY A NUMBER NEEDS A TREE

The sentence that is not a finding
"sales are down 8%" -> which driver moved? -> by how much? -> who owns it?

A top-line number tells you that something happened. It cannot tell you what, and it cannot be assigned to anyone.

A driver tree turns a number into a lever with an owner. That is the whole job.
What the tree has to survive

A branch that nobody owns cannot be actioned, and a branch that does not reconcile with its parent lets the diagnosis hide. Session b1 breaks one tree in the simulator until naming the guilty driver is a reflex, because reading a tree under pressure is a different skill from drawing one.

SEC-03 THE THREE PATTERNS

Not every metric multiplies. The moment one lands on your desk you should already know which shape it is.
x
multiplicativeA product of drivers. GMV = Traffic x Conversion x AOV.
+
sumParts that add up. Revenue split by region.
~
bridgeA balance carried forward. Active users this month starts from last month's.
Why the shape decides the diagnosis

In a product, one driver moving 10 percent moves the top line 10 percent. In a sum it moves it by its share. In a bridge the question is never the level at all, it is what came in and what left.

Why six and not one

The mechanics are identical in every domain. What changes is which drivers exist, how fast they respond, and how long the lag is before a lever shows up in the top line. Seeing the same tree in six rooms is what stops it being an ecommerce trick.

SEC-06 WHERE A TREE LIES TO YOU

The wrong shapeReading a bridge as if it were a product. The level was never the question. Ask: does this metric multiply, add, or carry forward?
A lever with no ownerA branch nobody is accountable for is decoration on a slide. Ask: whose name is on this node?
Lagging onlyEvery driver in the tree confirms what already happened. Ask: which of these could have warned us?
A branch that does not reconcileThe children do not account for the parent, so the diagnosis has somewhere to hide. Ask: do these add back up?
Sessions a5 and a6 make the tree a ritual rather than an artifact: the weekly business review reads it, the monthly leadership summit acts on it.
